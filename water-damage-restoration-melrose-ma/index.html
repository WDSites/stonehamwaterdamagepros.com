What a good rebuild waits for

Repair starts when the structure reads dry, not when it looks dry. A moisture meter gives numbers for drywall, wood framing and flooring, and a thermal imaging camera shows cool wet patches behind paint. Only then does it make sense to patch, prime and refinish. Ask any contractor, including us, for the dry readings before closing a wall.

Our water damage repair work covers the rebuild: drywall, insulation, trim, flooring and paint. Scope depends on how much material was wet, what it is made of and how long it stayed wet. Plaster, hardwood and old subfloor behave differently from new drywall, so the quote differs. We explain scope and price before work starts.

When water is spreading in Melrose, the clock on mold starts

Mold needs moisture, and it can start growing on damp materials within about 48 hours. That is why the first call matters even when the visible damage is small. A slow leak behind a tub surround or a wet ceiling below a bathroom can sit unseen while a patch of paint bubbles.

If growth has started, mold remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, using containment barriers, negative air, HEPA air scrubbers and removal of affected porous material. The source leak must be fixed first. Questions about health belong to your physician, and you should ask whether your state licenses mold work.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Cut the water feeding the leak

Close the fixture valve, the water heater line or the main, whichever stops the flow fastest. Write down which valve you used.

Shut breakers if the floor is dry

Turn off power to wet rooms only when you can reach the panel dry. If a ceiling light is full of water, leave it off and do not touch the switch.

Save the keepsakes

Photo albums, papers, rugs and upholstered furniture go to a dry upper room. Stack wooden furniture on blocks or plastic.

Hold everyone back from dirty water

If it came from a drain or sat for a day, keep children, pets and bare feet out. Gray or dark water should be treated as Category 2 or 3.

Drying before you rebuild

A day or two after the water stops, the surface can feel fine while the cavity behind it is wet. Air movers push air across surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of that air. Drying usually takes several days, and you check readings daily instead of guessing.

Our water mitigation crews set that equipment first so the rebuild does not trap moisture. If you plan to sell or refinance, a documented dry reading and any clearance testing give a buyer or lender something concrete. Ask your lender what documentation they require, because we do not speak for lenders.

Insurance language matters too. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage. Check your policy and photograph everything before cleanup.

How soon should I check for mold after a leak?

Look within a couple of days. Mold needs moisture and can start growing within about 48 hours on damp materials. Musty smells, bubbling paint and soft baseboards are signs. If you see growth, stop the water source first and ask about testing under the S520 standard.
