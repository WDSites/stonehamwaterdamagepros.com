Restoration Cost Guide

Water Damage Restoration Cost in Stoneham, MA: What Sets the Bill

No honest company can price a wet home from a phone call. Here is what moves the number and how to compare two written scopes.

Call (339) 333-9345

By the Stoneham Water Damage Pros team | Updated October 29, 2026

The final cost of water damage restoration depends on five things: the water category, how much area is wet, which materials soaked it up, how many days of drying equipment the job needs, and how much rebuilding follows. Because those are only known once someone measures the damage with moisture meters, a phone quote is a rough guess at best. A written scope, agreed before work starts, is what fixes the price.

Why a phone number is rarely exact

Water does not stay where it lands. It runs under baseboards, wicks up drywall and soaks into subfloor, so the wet area is nearly always larger than the stain you can see. A person on the phone cannot see inside your wall cavity.

What a company can do by phone is explain how it prices and what it will measure. If someone gives you a hard number sight unseen for anything beyond a small spill, ask what it assumes. The honest answer is a range of scenarios, not a single figure. This page gives no dollar amounts for that reason, because any figure printed here would be wrong for your home.

The factors that build the bill

Factor Why it changes the scope What to ask
Water category (1, 2 or 3) Clean supply water can often be dried in place. Gray or sewage water means removal, disinfection and protective gear. Which category is this, and how did you decide?
Affected area More wet square footage means more extraction, more air movers and more materials to inspect. Did you map the wet area with a meter or a thermal imaging camera?
Materials Drywall, carpet pad, hardwood, plaster and insulation each dry or fail differently. Which materials do you expect to save and which to remove?
Drying days and equipment Each day of air movers and LGR dehumidifiers is a line in the scope. How many days do you expect, and how will you confirm dry?
Rebuild scope Removed drywall, trim and flooring have to be replaced after drying. Is rebuild in this scope or separate?
Access and timing Finished basements, tight crawl spaces and after-hours calls change labor. Does the scope assume normal hours or an emergency call?
Permits and inspections Some repair work may need a permit and an inspection. Who pulls the permit, and what does it cost on top?

Water category, in plain terms

Category 1 water comes from a clean source, such as a supply line or an overflowing sink. Category 2 carries some contamination, like washing machine or dishwasher discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage and floodwater.

Category changes the scope more than most people expect. Clean water that has been sitting for a couple of days can slide toward a higher category, which is one reason speed matters for cost. The same flooded room can be a drying job if caught early and a tear-out job if it is not.

Drying is a number of days, not a flat task

Most water damage jobs run on drying time. After extraction, crews set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, then return each day to read moisture levels in drywall, framing and subfloor. Many straightforward jobs need around three to five days, though thick materials, hardwood and cool basements can take longer.

That daily monitoring is where a good scope earns its keep. It should list equipment counts, how often readings are taken, and the target for calling the structure dry. A company that works to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, will follow documented steps, and you can ask any company whether it does.

Mitigation versus rebuild

These are two different bills, and mixing them up is the most common source of confusion. Mitigation is the emergency part: stopping the water, extracting it, removing what cannot be saved and drying the rest. Our water mitigation work covers that stage.

Rebuild is putting the house back: new drywall, paint, trim, flooring. It often follows mitigation by days or weeks, and it can be done by the same company or by a contractor you choose. When you get a scope, check which stage it covers. A low mitigation number with rebuild left out is not comparable to a higher one that includes it.

How to compare two written scopes

If you have two estimates, do not compare the totals first. Compare what each one measures and promises to do.

  1. Line up the scope

    Check that both cover the same rooms, the same water category and the same stage, mitigation or rebuild.

  2. Compare the equipment

    Look at air mover and dehumidifier counts and the number of drying days each assumes.

  3. Check the monitoring

    Does each plan include daily moisture readings and a stated dry target?

  4. Look at the removal list

    Which materials are being cut out, and what is the reason for each?

  5. Ask about changes

    What happens to the price if more wet material turns up behind a wall?

  6. Confirm the rebuild line

    Is it included, separate or left to you? Is any permit mentioned?

A scope that says less is not cheaper. It is usually just less complete, and the missing items come back as add-ons.

When a price changes midway

A price can move for honest reasons. A thermal imaging camera may reveal a wet wall nobody could see, or a ceiling may turn out to be holding more water than it showed. What should not happen is a surprise. The scope should say how additions are handled and who approves them before any added work starts.

When to call a professional

Call as soon as you see standing water, wet drywall or a lingering damp smell, because the first 24 to 48 hours are when cost grows fastest. If the water is sewage or touched electrical equipment, stay out and call the utility or 911 before the restoration line. A small, clean spill on a sealed floor can be towel-dried. Anything that reached walls or subfloor needs meters.

Frequently asked questions

Can anyone tell me the cost over the phone?

Not accurately. A phone call can explain how pricing works, but the real number depends on measured moisture, the water category and the materials involved. Be cautious of any firm figure given without anyone seeing the damage, and ask what it assumes about area and drying days.

Why do two quotes for the same room differ so much?

Usually the scopes differ, not the prices. One may include more drying days, extra equipment, daily monitoring or the rebuild, while the other leaves them out. Line up the scopes item by item before judging the totals, and ask each company what is not included.

Does a higher water category cost more?

Generally yes. Category 2 and 3 water call for more protective gear, removal of porous materials and disinfection, where Category 1 may be dried in place. Clean water left for a day or two can also move into a higher category, so quick action keeps the scope smaller.

Is rebuild included in a restoration bill?

Not automatically. Mitigation covers stopping, extracting and drying, while rebuild covers replacing drywall, paint, trim and flooring. Ask whether the scope includes rebuild, whether it is a separate document, and whether you may use another contractor for that stage.

Do I need a permit for water damage repair in Stoneham?

The town page does not say when repairs need one. Stoneham's Building Department takes applications online and asks you to call for inspections, so phone the office with a short description of the work. Ask who files the permit, you or your contractor, and put the answer in the written scope.
