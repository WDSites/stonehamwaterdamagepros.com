Carpet Water Damage

Water Damaged Carpet Restoration in Stoneham, MA

Carpet, pad, tack strip and subfloor each react to water differently. The water category and the clock decide what stays.

Call (339) 333-9345

Stand on wet carpet and you feel only the top layer. Under it sits a pad that works like a sponge, a tack strip nailed to the floor and a subfloor that may be soaking up whatever drains through. Restoring carpet means dealing with all four layers.

Many Stoneham homes have finished basements with wall-to-wall carpet, and that is where this problem shows up most. A washer hose, a sump pump failure or seepage after heavy rain can wet an entire lower level before anyone is home to notice.

What the layers do when they get wet

The carpet fibers and backing are often synthetic and handle water well. The pad is usually foam or rebond and holds a lot of water for a long time. The tack strip is wood and can stain and swell. The subfloor, whether plywood, boards or concrete, is what the whole job depends on.

If the pad stays wet, it keeps the underside of the carpet damp, and smells and mold growth follow. Mold may begin to grow on damp materials after roughly two days. That is why drying the layer you cannot see matters as much as extracting the one you can.

Safety before you walk on it

Wet carpet and electricity do not mix. If water reached outlets, power strips or a panel in the room, stay out. Cut power only if the panel is reachable from dry floor, and put any gas or electrical hazard in the hands of the utility or 911 before you phone us.

If the water came from a toilet, a floor drain or a sewer backup, keep children and pets out and do not walk on it. Carpet touched by sewage is not something to extract yourself. A plumber stops the source, and we dispatch the cleanup.

Water category decides what can be saved

The most important question is where the water came from. Clean water from a supply line, in the first 24 to 48 hours, often leaves carpet that can be lifted, cleaned and dried. Gray water from a washer or dishwasher is a harder case. Black water, such as sewage or floodwater, means the carpet and pad come out.

Water category Typical source Usual result for the carpet
Category 1 Supply line, faucet overflow, melting ice maker line Carpet often saved, pad often replaced, subfloor dried
Category 2 Washer or dishwasher discharge, sump overflow Carpet may be saved after cleaning and antimicrobial, pad usually replaced
Category 3 Sewage backup, river or flood water, long-standing water Carpet and pad removed and discarded, subfloor cleaned and disinfected

Time shifts the category. Clean water that sits for days can degrade, and a pad that stayed wet that long may be removed even if the source was a pipe.

How a crew handles wet carpet

A technician first maps where the moisture is using meters and a thermal camera, then works through these steps.

  1. Extract the water

    A weighted extraction wand pulls water out of the carpet and, where it is safe, from the pad underneath.

  2. Lift and prop

    Carpet is released from the tack strip along a wall, folded back and propped so air can reach the pad and floor. Pad is removed when it cannot dry.

  3. Treat and clean

    Antimicrobial is applied to the back of salvageable carpet and to the exposed subfloor. Surface cleaning handles odor and stains.

  4. Dry with air movers

    Air movers push air under and across the carpet, while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Floor drying mats can speed up a stubborn spot.

  5. Reinstall or replace

    Dry carpet is stretched back onto new tack strips and new pad. Carpet that delaminated or shrank is replaced.

Delamination, stretching and odor

Carpet backing holds the fibers together with latex. If the carpet stays wet too long, the backing can separate from the fibers, which is called delamination. A stretched carpet that has dried can also ripple, which a stretcher can fix as long as the backing is intact.

Odor is a signal that something is still damp or soiled. A musty smell after drying points to pad, subfloor or a wall base that was missed. If mold is found, see mold remediation, since the water source has to be fixed first.

Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan lists flood events including March 29, 2010, when basements flooded, and says stormwater flooding can occur anywhere in town. The plan attributes that to undersized culverts and insufficient stormwater detention and drainage. If your finished basement is carpeted, assume heavy rain can reach it, and keep valuables off the floor.

The same plan marks several spots as locally identified flood areas, including Dike's Pond, Montvale Avenue, Main Street and the Franklin Street area. The FEMA flood map will show where your own address falls. Ground water and storm water are usually Category 3, which is why a flood damage restoration call differs from a leak call.

What changes the cost

The price tracks the water category (1, 2 or 3), how much carpet is wet, the thickness of the pad and how many rooms the water crossed. The days of drying and the number of air movers and dehumidifiers count too, as does whether you want the carpet saved or replaced. You see the scope and price before work begins.

Insurance fit

A washer hose that let go or a supply line that burst is the kind of sudden event insurers often cover. Slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flooding generally requires its own policy. Review your policy, speak with your insurer, and keep photos of the carpet before anything is lifted.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not leave the carpet down because it looks dry on top. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet, since it is not made for water and is an electrical risk. When choosing a company, find out if ANSI/IICRC S500, the published restoration standard, guides its crews.

Frequently asked questions

Can wet carpet be saved?

Often, when the water was clean and the carpet is extracted and dried within the first 24 to 48 hours. Gray water needs cleaning and antimicrobial treatment first. Sewage or flood water means the carpet is discarded. A crew checks the source and the age of the water before deciding.

Does the pad have to be replaced?

Usually. Pad holds water and dries slowly under carpet, so a crew commonly removes and replaces it even when the carpet is saved.

How long does carpet take to dry?

Most carpet and floors need several days of air movers and dehumidifiers, depending on the pad, the subfloor and the room. Dry readings, not a calendar, decide when the equipment leaves.

Can I dry the carpet myself with fans?

Fans help the top but cannot dry a pad or a concrete floor underneath. If you try, lift the carpet edge and prop it, and still have the pad checked. Wet pad left in place is where odors and mold start.

Will the carpet shrink or come apart?

It can. Wet carpet can delaminate or ripple, especially if it stayed wet for days. Stretching often fixes ripples, but a separated backing means replacement.

Does my insurance cover carpet water damage?

The cause settles it. A burst supply line is often covered as sudden damage, while slow leaks and flooding often are not. Talk to your insurer and read your policy before starting any work.
