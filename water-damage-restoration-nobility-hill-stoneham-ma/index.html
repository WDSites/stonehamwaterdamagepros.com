Nobility Hill, Stoneham

Water Damage Restoration in Nobility Hill, Stoneham, MA

Older wood-frame houses on a hilltop west of Central Square need careful drying before anyone repairs trim, floors or plaster.

Call (339) 333-9345

Nobility Hill is the hilltop pocket of older houses a few blocks west of Main Street and Central Square. The 1989 National Register nomination form, which the town posts on its website, places the district where Maple Street, Chestnut Street and Cedar Avenue meet. Cedar Avenue makes a short U between the other two. If water has reached a house there, this page covers what cleanup involves and what to do first.

Call (339) 333-9345 if you want to talk it through. Describe what is wet, when it started and what you think caused it.

The houses and the ground they sit on

According to the nomination form, most buildings in the district are one and a half to two and a half story wood-frame houses dating from the 1860s to the 1920s. The styles run from Italianate and Second Empire to Stick, Queen Anne, Colonial Revival and Dutch Colonial, with some mid-century ranch houses mixed in. The documented examples sit on brick or granite foundations.

The same form calls the area "high, well drained, hilly terraces." That describes the setting. It does not say your cellar is dry, and the form does not mention basements at all. If your house has a basement, a cellar or a crawl space, say so on the call, because it changes where water collects and how drying is set up.

What the record says about water here

Nothing we found documents flooding on the hill itself. Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan does list a June 24, 2008 storm when water rose on Maple Street and two cars were submerged. Maple Street forms the south edge of the district, and the plan does not say which stretch flooded. The same plan adds that stormwater can flood a street with no creek nearby.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Before assuming anything either way, find your own address on FEMA's flood map. Most water damage in a house like yours starts indoors anyway, from a supply line, an appliance, a roof or a ceiling above the room that got wet.

Plumbing and appliance leaks

A split supply line, a washer hose or a water heater fitting can soak floors and the ceiling below. See burst pipe cleanup for what happens after the water is shut off.

Roof and ceiling water

Water that enters at the roof can travel along framing before it shows as a stain. The roof leak page and the ceiling repair page cover both ends of that path.

Storm water

Wind-driven rain at windows and doors can wet walls and floors fast. Call as soon as you see it, because wood trim and floors take on water quickly.

How drying works in an older wood-frame house

Restoration professionals start with moisture meters on framing, subfloor, trim and flooring, then use a thermal imaging camera to see wet areas behind paint. Wet material that cannot be saved is cut out, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what remains. Readings are logged on each visit so the trend is on paper. The full method is explained on the structural drying page.

Original wood trim, floors and plaster are often worth saving, so a careful crew tests and dries before deciding what comes out. If your walls are plaster rather than drywall, mention it. Plaster, old paint and thick wood dry at different speeds, and the plan should reflect that. Rebuild work comes after readings return to normal, which is covered under water damage repair.

Safety first: the first hour

Look before you touch anything electrical. If water is near outlets, a panel or a ceiling light fixture, stay out of that room and use a dry hand and dry footing only if you must reach the panel. When in doubt, leave the breaker alone and call the utility or 911 for any sparking, burning smell or gas odor.

Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely. Keep people and pets out of standing water, because it can hide electrical hazards or carry contamination. Photograph the damage, move small valuables to a dry room and leave heavy furniture alone. A sagging ceiling can drop without warning, so stay out from under it.

Insurance and records

Photos, a list of what got wet and moisture readings can help describe the loss. An itemized scope of proposed work helps too. Whether your policy responds depends on the cause of the damage and the policy terms, so read your policy and ask your insurer. A sudden failure and a slow leak are often treated differently. No page, including this one, can tell you a claim will be approved.

A note for historic houses: if you are unsure whether any rules apply to exterior or structural repairs on your property, ask the Stoneham Building Department and the town's Historical Commission before work begins.

What affects the cost

Price depends on how many rooms are wet, which materials are involved, the water category, how many days of drying are needed and how much rebuilding follows. Older finishes and trim can take longer to match. Ask for the scope and price in writing before work starts. A fair question for any provider is how closely it follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard.

Mistakes to avoid

Skip the quick paint job over a stain, and never close up a wall that still reads wet. Do not run household fans at a wet plaster ceiling and call it dry. Do not wait on mold, which can begin on damp materials within about 48 hours. Do not pull up old flooring before someone has measured it, since boards that look ruined sometimes dry flat.

Frequently asked questions

Is water damage handled differently in an older Nobility Hill house?

The goals are the same, but the plan changes with the materials. Wood framing, trim, plaster and old finishes dry at different rates, so readings are taken on each material. Tell the caller the age of the house and what the walls are made of.

Is Nobility Hill in a FEMA flood zone?

No zone has been confirmed for the neighborhood as a whole, and this page does not guess. Flood zones are mapped by address, and a street can be partly in and partly out. Check your own property, then ask your insurer about flood coverage, which is separate from most home policies.

Can wood floors and trim be saved after a leak?

Often, when water is clean and drying starts quickly. Solid wood can cup and then flatten as it dries. Boards that buckle, split or stay cupped after drying are usually replaced. Measured readings, not appearance, should decide.

Do I need a permit for water damage repairs?

The town's website does not say if repair after a leak needs a permit, so ask first. The Building Department office sits on the garden level at 35 Central Street. Applications are filed online and inspections are booked by phone. Do this before framing, electrical or larger rebuild work.

What should I photograph before cleanup starts?

Take wide shots of each wet room, close shots of stains, the source of the leak if it is visible and any wet belongings. Date stamps help. Only do this when the area is safe to enter.
