Commercial Water Damage

Commercial Property Water Damage Restoration in Stoneham, MA

When water hits a business space, the clock runs on inventory, records and operating hours. Here is how a restoration dispatch handles it.

Call (339) 333-9345

The same pipe break that ruins a kitchen at home raises more questions in a shop, office or shared building: who owns the pipe, who holds the lease, what stock is on the floor and when the doors must open again.

This page covers what we actually do: dispatch licensed and insured restoration professionals for extraction, drying, cleanup and written documentation after a pipe break, a sprinkler line leak, a roof leak or storm water getting inside. It does not cover fixing the pipe, the sprinkler system or the roof. Those belong to a plumber, a fire protection contractor or a roofer.

Safety first when a business space floods

Before anything else, get people out of wet areas. If water has reached outlets, panels, lighting or equipment, stay clear. Cut power only when the panel can be reached from dry flooring. If there is a gas smell or an electrical hazard, the utility or 911 comes first and our line second.

Then call the building owner or property manager, since they usually control the main shut-off and the sprinkler riser. If a sprinkler line is discharging, do not try to cap it yourself. Shut off the water to the suite or building at the valve your manager identifies, and keep staff and customers out of standing water.

Who to call, and in what order

The order depends on what is leaking and where.

  1. Make the space safe

    Clear people out, avoid electrical hazards and note any gas smell. Call 911 or the utility for those risks first.

  2. Stop the water

    Reach the property manager, landlord or maintenance contact and close the nearest shut-off. A plumber or sprinkler contractor handles the actual repair.

  3. Call the restoration line

    Describe the space, the source and what is wet. We dispatch a crew and walk through what happens next. Same-day response when available.

  4. Tell your insurer

    Report the loss and ask what your policy needs for documentation. Do this early, not after repairs.

What happens once a crew arrives

A technician starts with a walk-through carrying moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, tracing how far the water went. In a commercial building, that often means checking the floor below, the ceiling cavity above and shared walls next door, beyond the room where the water showed up.

Extraction units pull standing water off carpet, tile and concrete. Wet carpet tile, ceiling tile, insulation and baseboard that cannot dry in place are removed in a controlled way. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the remaining structure, and the crew logs readings each visit. You can read more on how that works under structural drying and the initial response under emergency water removal.

Protecting inventory and records

In general terms, move what you can off the floor and away from wet walls before the crew arrives. Boxes of paper, electronics and stock sitting on concrete wick moisture fast. Photograph them where they sit, then lift them onto shelves or tables.

Do not try to dry paper records or electronics yourself with heaters or hair dryers. Tell the crew what is valuable so it can be set aside, and ask your insurer what documentation it wants for stock and equipment.

Working around operating hours

Many businesses cannot close. A crew can often work in sections, dry an affected area behind containment barriers, and schedule noisy or disruptive steps outside opening hours when the space allows it.

Some spaces cannot stay open safely. If the floor is wet, power is compromised or the water was contaminated, closing the area is the right call.

Landlord, tenant and the building's systems

Who pays depends on the lease, the source of the water and your policy, none of which we can decide for you. In general terms, the building owner often handles the structure and shared systems, while a tenant often handles contents, improvements and business interruption coverage. Read your lease and call both insurers if there is doubt.

Record the time you found the water, the source and the rooms hit. The crew adds moisture readings and a list of materials taken out, so the file is complete when an adjuster or property owner asks.

Older storefronts and mixed-use blocks around the Common are covered on the Central Square page.

Permits for a commercial rebuild

Permit applications in Stoneham go through the town's online portal, and the Building Department at 35 Central Street, Garden Level, wants a call for any inspection. Because its page leaves open when water damage repair needs a permit, ask before you rebuild walls, ceilings or finishes in a commercial space.

What changes the cost

The number moves with the water category (1, 2 or 3), how many square feet are wet, the flooring and ceiling types, how many floors the water crossed, machine count and drying days, and how much demolition is needed. After-hours access, containment and phased work can change the scope too. Scope and price are spelled out before the work starts.

Insurance fit

A burst supply line or a discharged sprinkler head is a sudden, accidental event, and those are often covered. Slow leaks left unattended often are not, and floodwater typically needs its own flood policy. Ask your insurer how contents, the building and business interruption are treated.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not wait for the landlord to decide, since a wet ceiling tile or carpet tile keeps absorbing water while you wait. Do not throw out damaged stock before photographing it. Do not let anyone start rebuild before the structure reads dry. Ask any contractor you call whether it follows ANSI/IICRC S500, a published guide to water damage restoration work.

Frequently asked questions

Can you help with water damage in a shared building or a single suite?

Yes. We dispatch crews for a single suite or a shared building. Tell us who the property manager is and where the shut-off is, since the building owner usually controls both and has to approve work on common areas.

Do you fix the broken pipe or sprinkler line?

No. A licensed plumber or sprinkler contractor stops and repairs the source. Our part is extraction, drying, cleanup and documentation of what the water touched, and we can coordinate timing so the repair and the drying do not block each other.

Can the business stay open during drying?

Sometimes, in sections. It depends on how wet the floor is, whether power is safe and whether the water was clean. A crew can use containment barriers and schedule disruptive work outside opening hours when the space allows it.

Who is responsible, the landlord or the tenant?

It depends on the lease, the cause and the policies in place. In general terms, the owner covers the building and shared systems, and the tenant covers contents and improvements. Read your lease and ask both insurers before assuming.

What records should I keep after a commercial water loss?

Photos and short videos of the damage before cleanup, the time you found it, the source, a list of affected stock or equipment and any receipts. Your crew adds moisture readings and a list of materials removed.
