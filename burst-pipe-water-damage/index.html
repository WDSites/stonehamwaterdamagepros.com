How a pipe lets go inside the house

Most burst pipe calls are not dramatic bursts at all. A braided washer hose lets go at the coupling, a supply line under a vanity cracks at the nut, or a copper joint inside a wall cavity weeps and then fails under pressure. Household water pressure is constant, so a small failure keeps pushing water out.

A split in a wall or ceiling cavity can run a long time before it shows. If the cause is cold weather, the frozen pipe page covers that case, because the damage pattern and the thaw timing are different.

Water spreading right now: safety before you touch anything

Water and electricity do not mix, and a ceiling that is holding water can come down without warning. Keep everyone out of rooms where water drips from light fixtures, and stay clear of any bulging ceiling.

Safety first: electrical and gas

If water reached outlets, the panel, or a gas appliance, do not enter the wet area. Cut the breaker if the walk to the panel is dry. Sparking, a buzzing outlet or the smell of gas calls for the utility or 911 before anyone dials the restoration line. Stay out of standing water near a heater or a washer.

What you are seeing and what it means

The signs of a failed pipe tell you where to look next. Wet drywall that feels soft or cold, a stain spreading on a ceiling, bubbling paint and a musty smell in a closed room all point to water inside a cavity.

What you see What it usually means
Water dripping from a ceiling light or vent A supply line above is leaking and water is running along joists
Wet wall base and a stain climbing the drywall A line inside the wall is open, and the wall is wicking upward
Wet basement ceiling below a bathroom Water traveled down through the floor and the subfloor is saturated

Where the water goes next

Water follows gravity and framing. A break on the second floor can run down a wall cavity, soak the insulation, drip through the first floor ceiling and pool in the basement. Each floor it crosses adds wet drywall, wet insulation and wet subfloor.

That is why a thermal imaging camera and moisture meters matter. A room may look dry while the wall behind it holds water. If the water has spread across several floors, emergency water removal comes first to pump and extract it.

Our process after a burst pipe

  1. Assess and map

    A crew walks the wet areas with you, checks surfaces with moisture meters, and scans walls and ceilings with a thermal imaging camera. The water category and the affected materials are noted.

  2. Extract

    Extraction units pull standing water from floors and carpet. Weighted tools press water out of carpet and pad when they can be saved.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Saturated insulation, soaked pad and swollen drywall are removed. Crews may cut small openings in walls to reach wet cavities.

  4. Dry and monitor

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers stay in place while crews compare moisture readings with a dry room in your own home, adjusting the equipment on each return visit.

  5. Clean and treat

    Surfaces that stay are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial before the rebuild starts.

A plumber stops the leak and replaces the damaged pipe or valve. We do not repair plumbing. The street main versus your house

Before you call a plumber, check whether the problem is inside your house or out on the line. The town's water quality report describes a supply from the MWRA, delivered by aqueduct from the Quabbin Reservoir. In practice, water gets to your kitchen tap through a street main and then a service line running to your meter.

A leak that continues once your own valve is shut may sit on the town side. The Public Works Water/Sewer Department page counts roughly 75 miles of mains and around 6,100 service connections, so the network is large. Outside business hours, that same page sends emergencies to the Stoneham Police Department line. Report a suspected main break there and call us for the cleanup inside.

What changes the cost

Nothing starts until the scope and price have been explained, so you can decide with the details in front of you.

Water category: clean supply line water is lighter work than water that sat or mixed with other sources. Affected area: the number of rooms and floors the water crossed. Materials: carpet, pad, plaster, drywall and flooring each need different handling. Drying days and equipment: more cavities mean more air movers and dehumidifiers running longer. Rebuild scope: replacing drywall, trim and flooring is a separate phase from drying.

Insurance and documentation

Insurers usually treat a pipe that fails suddenly differently from one that has dripped for months. A fresh supply line failure often falls under a homeowner policy, a long-running seep often does not, and rising groundwater needs its own flood product. Read the policy language, then put your questions to the insurer.

Take photos of the source and every damaged room before anything is removed, and keep the failed part if you can.

Mistakes to avoid

Waiting to see if it dries. Wet drywall and insulation can start growing mold on damp materials within about 48 hours. Running a household fan and calling it done. Fans move air, but they do not pull moisture out of closed cavities. Shutting only the fixture valve. Close the main if you cannot find the source. Turning the water back on before a plumber checks the repair.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency burst pipe water damage cleanup available in Stoneham?

Yes. Dispatch picks up day and night, weekends and holidays included. Say where the water started and which rooms are wet, and you will hear safety steps while a crew is lined up. Many calls get a crew that day when one is open, but no arrival time is promised.

Do I need to find the break before calling you?

No. Close the main shut-off to stop the flow and call. A plumber finds and fixes the pipe, and we handle the water that already got out.

How long does drying take after a pipe break?

It varies with the number of wet cavities, the materials and the humidity. Many jobs need several days of air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, with daily readings. We never promise a set number of days.

Does my homeowner policy cover a burst pipe?

A pipe that fails suddenly is the kind of event many policies address, and a slow drip over months is often treated another way. Wording varies, so read yours or call your insurer. Take photos of everything before cleanup begins.
