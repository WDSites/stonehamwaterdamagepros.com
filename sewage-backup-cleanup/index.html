Sewage Backup Cleanup

Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Stoneham, MA

Wastewater from a floor drain or toilet is category 3 water. Containment, protective gear and disinfection come before anything else.

Call (339) 333-9345

Raw sewage on a basement floor is a different job from a leaking pipe. The water carries bacteria and waste, so it is treated as category 3, the most contaminated class in the water damage standards. Do not wade in, do not mop it, and do not run a household wet vac through it.

Calls are answered around the clock. Crews are dispatched the same day whenever one is on shift, though no one can name an arrival time in advance. While you wait, close the door to the affected room, keep children and pets well away, and stop using every toilet, sink, shower and washing machine in the house.

Sewage coming up right now: safety before you touch anything

Sewage and electricity make a bad pair. If the water is reaching outlets, a furnace, a freezer or the electrical panel, stay out. Throw the main breaker only if you can walk to the panel on dry ground. A gas smell or an electrical hazard goes to the utility or 911, and that call comes ahead of ours.

Wash your hands after any contact, and take off shoes and clothing that touched the area. Questions about exposure belong to a physician.

How sewage gets into a house

Most backups start in one of two places. The first is your own line, the private lateral that runs from the house to the town sewer. A grease blockage, roots, a collapsed section or a belly in the pipe can stop flow, and wastewater has nowhere to go but up through the lowest opening in the house. That is usually a basement floor drain, a laundry tub or a toilet.

The second is the town main. Heavy rain pushes extra water into sewer lines, and when the main fills, it can push back toward connected homes. Stoneham's Public Works page says the department is working to cut sewer infiltration and inflow, meaning the groundwater and stormwater that seeps into sewer pipes. That is a reason to notice whether neighbors are having the same problem.

Whose pipe is it? Who to call first

The quickest way to sort this out is to look at what is happening around you. If only your house is backing up, the blockage is likely in your line, and a licensed plumber or drain contractor is the person to clear it. If your neighbors report the same thing, or several fixtures back up at once, report it to the town.

The Stoneham Public Works Water/Sewer Department counts 5 pumping stations serving roughly 75 miles of sewer line. After business hours, the department's page routes emergencies through the Stoneham Police Department line. Make that call, then call us for the cleanup. We do not clear drains or repair sewer lines. We handle the contaminated materials the backup left behind.

What the cleanup looks like

  1. Assess and set up protection

    The crew puts on protective gear, then maps the contamination with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, marking how far the contamination traveled. Containment barriers go up to keep dust and spray out of clean rooms.

  2. Extract the wastewater

    Extraction units pull standing sewage out of the room. The liquid goes into sealed containers or a truck, never into your yard or a storm drain.

  3. Remove what cannot be cleaned

    Carpet, pad, insulation, the lower part of drywall, and other porous materials that soaked up sewage are cut out and bagged. Cleaning them is not reliable, so they go.

  4. Clean and disinfect

    Concrete, framing and other hard surfaces are scrubbed, then treated with a disinfectant or antimicrobial product. Stored items are sorted into wash, wipe or discard.

  5. Dry and scrub the air

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry out the structure, and HEPA air scrubbers with negative air keep airborne particles from reaching the rest of the house.

Why porous materials come out

Water category is about what the water carried, not how it looks. Sewage soaks into drywall paper, carpet backing, wood subfloor edges and upholstery, and it cannot be rinsed back out. Leaving those materials in place leaves the contamination in place, and a lingering odor is the sign.

Hard, sealed surfaces are a different story. A poured concrete floor, a metal duct or a sealed framing member can usually be cleaned and disinfected and kept. Your crew decides item by item, and you can ask to see readings and photos for any call.

What changes the cost

You receive the scope and a price ahead of the work, once the crew has looked at the space. These factors drive the number:

How far the sewage spread and how many rooms and floors it touched.

The amount of porous material that has to be removed and disposed of.

Protective gear, containment barriers and the number of days HEPA air scrubbers and dehumidifiers run.

Whether the lowest floor is finished, since carpet, drywall and trim add removal and rebuild work.

What needs to be replaced afterward, which is a separate repair phase.

Insurance basics

Coverage for sewage backup varies. Many standard homeowner policies exclude it unless an endorsement was added, and flood coverage is a separate matter. Sudden versus gradual causes may also change the answer. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph everything before it is removed.

Mistakes to avoid

Running the washing machine or flushing to see whether the drain has cleared.

Using a household wet vac, which can spray contaminated mist and ruin the motor.

Pouring bleach into standing sewage, which does not make it safe to handle.

Letting children or pets wander into the room.

Waiting. Damp porous materials can start growing mold within about 48 hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Stoneham?

Yes, around the clock, nights and holidays included. If a crew is on shift, dispatch can usually send it out that day, though nobody promises an arrival time. Say what is coming up, which rooms are wet and whether electrical equipment sits near the water. The dispatcher then talks you through what to do until the crew shows.

Can I clean up a small sewage backup myself?

It is not recommended. Even a small amount is category 3 water, and the materials it touched may need to be cut out rather than wiped. Protective gear, disinfectant and proper disposal matter. If you decide to deal with a very small spill, wear waterproof gloves and boots, and ask a professional what is safe to keep.

Does my plumber or the town fix the line?

A plumber or drain contractor handles a blocked private lateral. If the problem is in the town main, Public Works handles it. We do not clear drains or repair pipes. Our work begins after the flow is restored, with extraction, removal of contaminated materials, disinfection and drying.

What happens to my carpet and drywall?

Carpet, pad and insulation that touched sewage are almost always removed. Drywall is usually cut out above the wet line and replaced. Hard surfaces like concrete and framing are cleaned and disinfected. The crew explains the plan before work begins.

How long does the job take?

It depends on how far the sewage spread and how much has to come out. Removal and disinfection can move quickly, but drying with air movers and dehumidifiers takes several days. Rebuilding is a separate phase. Your crew gives an expected timeline with the scope and revises it if readings change.
