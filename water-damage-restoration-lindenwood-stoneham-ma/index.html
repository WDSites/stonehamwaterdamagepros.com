Lindenwood, Stoneham

Water Damage Restoration in Lindenwood, Stoneham, MA

Homes, condos and apartments in west Stoneham sit near Maple Street, Montvale Avenue and Sweetwater Brook, so storm water and indoor leaks both matter.

Lindenwood is in the western part of Stoneham. The town's 2012 preservation plan for the Old Burying Ground says Lindenwood Cemetery, opened in 1861, lies in the western part of town. This page is for people there who have water in the house, and it keeps to what the public record shows.

Where Lindenwood sits and who lives there

Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan describes a block group along Sweetwater Brook that includes Lindenwood Cemetery, residential housing, commercial and warehouse uses along Maple Street and Montvale Avenue and some residential apartments. A real estate neighborhood guide, which is a marketing source and not a town record, describes mostly Colonials, Capes, ramblers and split-levels, plus condo buildings near the edges. It gives a median build year of 1956.

So expect a mix. A single-family house with a basement, a condo with a unit above and an apartment building each call for a different plan. Say which one you have when you call.

What the town hazard plan says about water

The plan lists a floodplain between Montvale Avenue and Maple Street, along Sweetwater Brook, among the locally identified flood areas. Its FEMA discussion describes a mapped flood zone and a regulatory floodway along Montvale Avenue on the west side of town. A zone mapped along one corridor does not mean every nearby home is inside it, and a home outside any zone can still get wet.

Other records are town wide. The plan lists June 24, 2008, when Maple Street flooded and two cars were submerged, and March 29, 2010, when streets were closed and basements flooded. It adds that stormwater flooding is possible anywhere in town and links it to undersized culverts and insufficient detention. It also states that no repetitive flood loss buildings exist in Stoneham.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Then check your own address on FEMA's flood map and ask your insurer which flood coverage you hold.

Safety first: the first hour

Do not wade into water near electrical equipment. If the basement has water over outlets, a furnace, a water heater or the panel, stay upstairs and call the utility or 911. Never flip a breaker while standing in water. Keep children and pets away from standing water, which can carry contamination.

Shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe and you can reach it dry. Do not use fans, ordinary vacuums or DIY drying on sewage or storm runoff. Photograph damage when it is safe, and protect belongings only if the area is stable.

Rain, runoff and a full sump pit can bring water into a lower level. The flood damage restoration page explains extraction and drying, and storm water damage covers what comes next.

Houses, condos and apartments

In a house, water usually collects in the lowest level or runs down from a leak above. In a condo or apartment, a failed fixture in one unit can wet ceilings and walls in several others. If another unit caused your damage, document yours, tell the building manager and keep a record of the call. A water heater leak is one example that crosses floors.

Town sewer facts help explain backups. The Public Works Water/Sewer Department page lists about 75 miles of sewer and five sewer pumping stations, and says the department works to reduce sewer infiltration and inflow. If only your fixtures back up, the problem is often in your own line. If several neighbors see the same thing, the town should be told.

How drying works

Restoration professionals extract water, test wet materials with moisture meters and use a thermal imaging camera to find water behind surfaces. Wet insulation and soaked carpet pad are often removed. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the rest, and readings are logged each visit. The goal is to match dry readings from an unaffected part of your home.

Insurance and records

Photos, a written list of damaged items, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the loss. Whether anything is covered depends on the cause and the policy. Flood and plumbing damage are often treated differently, and a sudden event can be treated differently from a slow leak. Read your policy and ask your insurer, because no page can say what yours will pay.

What affects the cost

Price follows the size of the wet area, the materials, the water category, the days of drying and the rebuild that follows. Expect a clear scope and price before any work starts. If you compare providers, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference worth asking about. Woburn, which lies west of Stoneham, has its own page for Woburn water damage.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not assume the water is clean because it came from rain. Do not run a regular vacuum on standing water. Do not leave wet carpet or insulation in place for days. Do not decide the damage is minor before moisture has been measured, since mold can start on damp material within about 48 hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lindenwood in a FEMA flood zone?

That is not verified for the neighborhood as a whole. The hazard plan describes a mapped zone and floodway along Montvale Avenue, but maps apply by address. Check your property and confirm your flood coverage with your insurer.

Does Lindenwood flood often?

The public record does not show a repeat flooding pattern for the neighborhood. The plan lists town wide events and one floodplain near Montvale Avenue and Maple Street, and it reports no repetitive flood loss buildings in Stoneham.

What should I do if my basement takes on water after a storm?

Stay out if there is any electrical risk, keep people away from the water and photograph it from a safe spot. Then call for help with extraction and drying. Treat water that came across the ground as contaminated.

Who handles a leak that came from the unit above?

Start by telling the building manager or owner. Document your damage and keep a record of calls. Who pays depends on the lease, the cause and each policy, so ask your insurer.

Do I need a permit for repairs after water damage?

The town's page is silent on this for water damage repair. The Building Department, at 35 Central Street on the Garden Level, accepts permit applications online and books inspections by phone.
