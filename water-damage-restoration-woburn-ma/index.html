When water is spreading in Woburn, think about stairs and walls

Water goes down before it goes out. A supply line that fails on the second floor of a Woburn two-family feeds into the wall cavity, drops into the ceiling below, then pools on the floor. By the time you see a stain, the insulation inside has been wet for a while.

For water damage cleanup, the first job is finding where the water ended up, not where it appeared. We use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, then open the smallest access holes needed. Drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers usually takes several days, and equipment readings decide when it stops.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Stop the flow at its valve

Look for the shutoff under the sink, behind the toilet or at the water heater. If you can't find one, close the main where the line comes into the building.

Breakers off, but only from dry footing

Turn off power to affected rooms only if you can reach the panel without wading. A panel in a wet basement means you leave it and call 911 or the utility.

Clear the floor

Move electronics, paper files, rugs and soft furniture onto higher surfaces. In an office, unplug computers if you can do it safely and lift file boxes off the carpet.

Warn the neighbors and keep clear

Tell anyone in the units below or beside you. Keep children and pets out of standing water, which may carry bacteria from drains or old pipes.

Supply, wells and your shutoff

The Woburn Department of Public Works supplies water in conjunction with the MWRA to about 12,500 customers and operates the municipal well field at Horn Pond. That mix is useful to know when something goes wrong. If water pressure drops or turns cloudy across your street, it may be a system issue and your DPW is the first call. If it only happens in your building, the problem is behind your meter.

Know where your own main shutoff is before you need it. In a larger building, ask the property manager now, not during a flood. For long-term damage prevention, water mitigation crews can tell you where moisture lingers after a repair, so you are not guessing about hidden damp.

Burlington is a neighboring city. If your building is close to the line and you are unsure which side it falls on, call and give the street, and the dispatcher will sort it out.

Who do I call if the water problem is the city's, not mine?

Start with the Woburn Department of Public Works, which supplies water along with the MWRA. If water is only wet inside your building and the street is dry, the cause is probably your plumbing. If you smell gas or see sparks, call 911 and the utility before anyone else.

Do you handle commercial water damage in Woburn?

Yes. Offices, shops and multi-unit buildings need a moisture map first, then extraction and structured drying. Tell the dispatcher how much floor area is wet and whether tenants are affected. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Does the Horn Pond well field affect my basement?

The well field is a municipal water source, not a flood source. If your basement takes water after heavy storms, the cause is more likely grading, drainage or a failed sump pump. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage.
