Pick the step you are in

Water damage moves in a sequence. The source is stopped, standing water is removed, the structure is dried, wet materials are cleaned out, and only then are repairs made. Mold can enter the sequence anywhere moisture stays. If you are unsure where you are, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Water mitigation

Stops the damage in the first 24 to 72 hours with extraction, drying equipment and moisture mapping. Emergency water mitigation.

Emergency water removal

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from a burst pipe, a failed sump or a flooded corner. Emergency water removal.

Water damage cleanup

Removes wet debris and contaminated materials, then sanitizes and deodorizes. Water damage cleanup.

Flood damage restoration

Handles storm, groundwater and sewage flooding, with safety steps for dirty water. Flood damage restoration.

Water damage repair

Rebuilds drywall, insulation, flooring and finishes once drying is confirmed. Water damage repair.

Mold remediation

Contains and removes mold after a leak, with testing and clearance explained. Mold remediation.

Choosing

Which page matches your problem

If water is on the floor right now, read about emergency water removal and call. If the water is gone but walls and floors still feel damp, water mitigation covers the drying. A flooded basement after a storm usually points to flood damage restoration, and a musty smell or dark spots after a leak points to mold remediation.

Standards

What to ask any restoration company

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration and S520 is the one for mold remediation. Ask any company, including us, whether its crews work to them. The crews we dispatch are licensed and insured, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.
