Water Heater Leaks

A water heater rarely fails all at once. It weeps at a fitting, drips from the relief valve or seeps from a rusted tank bottom for days, and then one morning there is an inch of water on the floor. The tank holds a lot of water, and a failed bottom lets all of it out.

Your first job is to stop the heater from making things worse. Do not step into standing water around it. Once the heater is shut down and the cold supply is closed, call (339) 333-9345 to get extraction and drying moving.

Leaking right now: safety before you reach for the valve

A water heater sits at the crossing of three hazards: electricity, gas and very hot water. Treat the area as unsafe until each one is dealt with.

Safety first: power, gas and scalding water

Electric heater: switch off its breaker, but only when the floor between you and the panel is dry. Gas heater: if you smell gas, leave the house and call the utility or 911 before calling the restoration line. Hot water from the tank or relief valve can scald, so keep children and pets away from the leak.

If the panel is in the same wet basement, do not wade in. Call the utility or an electrician to cut power.

Shutting it down in the right order

Once the area is safe, work from the heater outward.

  1. Turn off the fuel or power

    Set a gas heater control to off or the pilot position. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater.

  2. Close the cold water supply

    Turn the valve on the cold inlet pipe above the tank. If it is stuck or leaks, close the main water valve instead.

  3. Let the tank cool

    Water inside can be very hot. Wait before touching fittings or opening drain valves.

  4. Photograph the heater and the floor

    Take pictures of the leak point, the label with the model and the wet area before anything is moved.

Where the leak comes from

Not every heater leak means a dead tank. The source decides what happens next.

A plumber replaces the heater or repairs the fitting. We do not do plumbing or install heaters. We dispatch water damage restoration professionals for the water that got out, and for the walls, floors and stored items it reached.

What gets wet around a heater

Heaters sit in basements, utility closets or garages. Water spreads along the floor and wicks into whatever is touching it. The wall base is the first casualty, because drywall draws water upward from the bottom edge. Cardboard boxes, stored holiday items, paint cans, laundry baskets and insulation on nearby pipes all take on moisture quickly.

A floor drain only moves water that reaches it, and a clogged drain can back up. Concrete slabs hold moisture, so a floor that looks dry on top may stay damp for days.

How crews clean up a heater leak

Tank water is usually clean (category 1) at the start, but it picks up dirt from the floor within a day or two.

Extraction units remove standing water first. Crews then scan with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, locating wet drywall, baseboards and subfloor. Wet insulation and swollen trim come out, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in until readings match the dry areas of your home.

Surfaces that stay are treated with an antimicrobial. The water damage cleanup page explains how removal and sanitizing are sorted, and mold remediation applies if growth has already started.

Insurance, permits and replacement

A tank that fails in a day is the sort of event homeowner policies often address. One that has seeped for weeks may be classed as a gradual leak, which is commonly excluded, and the heater itself usually sits outside a water damage claim. Policies differ, so read yours and ask your insurer.

A new heater can involve plumbing and gas work, so permit questions go to the Building Department, found on the Garden Level at 35 Central Street. Its online portal takes the applications, and inspections are scheduled by phone. Because the department's page does not say when a permit is required for water damage repair, ask the office directly.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

Check the area around the heater twice a year for rust, drips and damp cardboard. Do not store paper or fabric on the floor next to the tank. Do not ignore a relief valve that drips. Ask a plumber to check it. Do not turn a heater back on after a leak until a plumber has looked at it.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water heater leak cleanup available in Stoneham?

Yes. Dispatch is staffed day and night, including weekends and holidays. Tell us where the heater is and what got wet, and you will get safety steps while a crew is arranged. Whether a crew can come that same day depends on who is open, and we cannot promise a time.

Should I turn off the power or the gas first?

Shut off whichever you can reach safely. If you smell gas, get out of the house and phone the utility or 911 before anything else. If the floor is wet near the panel, do not walk in. Close the cold supply once the area is safe.

Is water from a leaking tank dangerous?

It can be, for two reasons. Hot water can scald, and standing water near electrical equipment is an electrical hazard. The water itself starts out clean, but it picks up dirt as it sits, so call early.

Who replaces the water heater after it leaks?

A plumber does. We handle the extraction, drying and cleanup of the water that got out. Once the area is dry, your plumber can install a new heater without working in a wet space.

Will my insurance pay for the damage?

A sudden tank failure is often covered, and a slow leak that went on for weeks often is not. Wording differs by policy, so read yours or call your insurer. Photograph the heater and the wet area before cleanup.
