Kitchen Water Damage

Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Stoneham, MA

Water collects inside cabinet bases, under flooring and behind walls. We dry the whole kitchen and sort what stays from what goes.

Your kitchen floor looks fine, but the cabinet under the sink feels damp and the toe kick has a faint wave in it. That is how most kitchen water damage begins. The visible part is small, and the wet part is hidden inside the cabinet base, under the flooring and inside the wall behind the sink.

Kitchens are built from layers that trap water: cabinet boxes, finished floor, subfloor and drywall.

How kitchen water damage happens

The sink is the usual origin. Supply lines, the disposal, the drain trap and the sprayer hose all sit inside a closed cabinet where a drip can run for days unseen. Dishwasher and refrigerator leaks are covered under appliance leak water damage. Pipes inside the wall behind the sink can also split, sending water down into the cabinet base.

Cabinet boxes made of particleboard soak up water like a sponge and swell at the bottom edge. Plywood handles it better, but glue lines and toe kicks still suffer. Water also moves sideways under the flooring.

What you see and what it means

Toe kick swelling or peeling

The cabinet base has been wet, and moisture is likely inside the box.

Cupped or lifted flooring near the sink

Water traveled under the finish layer.

Cracked grout or loose tile

The subfloor underneath is soft or shifting.

Avoid

Dark staining on the underside of the sink cabinet: a slow leak has been present for some time.

Musty smell when you open the cabinet

Damp material, and possibly mold growth beginning.

Ceiling stain in the room below

The water went through the floor system.

Safety before you touch it

Kitchens combine water with outlets, a dishwasher, a disposal and often a gas range. Shut off the water at the valve under the sink, or at the house main if you cannot reach it. Do not stand in water and operate switches. Where water sits near outlets or an appliance, power can come off at the panel, but only if your footing there is dry.

If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else. Call the restoration line after any electrical or gas risk has been handled. A plumber repairs the supply, drain or fitting. We do not do plumbing work.

Our process, cabinet to cavity

  1. Find the wet areas

    Moisture meters read cabinet bases, flooring and wall surfaces. A thermal imaging camera picks up temperature differences that show where water is hiding.

  2. Extract and open up

    Extraction units pull standing water from floors and cabinet bottoms. Toe kicks come off, and small access holes are made in wall bases so air can reach the cavity.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Swollen particleboard, soaked insulation, delaminated underlayment and wet drywall are cut out in a controlled way, with the edges kept clean for the rebuild.

  4. Dry in place

    Fans, dehumidifiers and specialty drying mats stay on the job for days. Readings are taken daily and equipment is moved as areas dry.

  5. Treat and clean

    Surfaces that stay get an antimicrobial treatment. Debris is bagged, and the work areas are cleaned for the next trade.

What can be saved and what usually goes

Solid wood and plywood cabinet boxes often dry in place when the water was clean and the response was prompt. Particleboard boxes that swelled usually come out, because the material does not recover its strength once it has expanded. Doors and drawer fronts are often fine even when the base is not.

Tile on a sound subfloor can often be dried through the grout lines or from below. Vinyl, laminate and engineered flooring over a wet subfloor are more likely to be lifted. You get a list of what stays and what goes before removal begins.

Water category

A clean supply line is the lightest case, while dishwasher or drain water adds cleaning and treatment.

Affected area

The number of cabinets, floor area and wall cavities that took on water.

Materials

Particleboard, solid wood, tile, laminate and drywall each need different handling.

Insurance fit

Insurers commonly view a supply line that suddenly fails as an accidental loss. A leak that ran under a sink for months often counts as gradual, which many policies exclude. Flood coverage does not apply to indoor plumbing. Read your policy, call your insurer, and photograph the cabinet, floor and failed part before anything is removed.

Mistakes to avoid

Avoid

Leaving the cabinet doors shut after a leak, which traps moisture.

Avoid

Putting new flooring or a new toe kick over damp wood.

Avoid

Painting or caulking over staining before the cavity is dry.

Avoid

Waiting on a "small" under-sink drip, since wet particleboard and subfloor get worse each week.

Frequently asked questions

Can water damaged kitchen cabinets be dried and saved?

Sometimes. Plywood and solid wood boxes often dry in place after clean water. Particleboard that has swelled usually has to be replaced, because it does not regain its strength. Doors and drawer fronts can often be kept even if a base cabinet is removed. A moisture check shows which case you have.

Should I pull out the toe kick myself?

You can remove it carefully to let air in, but do not push a household fan at it and walk away. Behind it, a crew can read moisture and see temperature patterns with meters and a thermal camera. Take photos first.

How long does a kitchen take to dry?

Plan on a number of days, which shifts with materials and depth. Cabinet cavities and subfloor dry slower than the surface. Daily readings decide when drying ends, and no day count is promised.

Will the flooring under the cabinets be wet too?

Often yes. Water spreads sideways under the cabinets before it shows up at the edge. Tile can sometimes be dried in place, while laminate and vinyl over a wet subfloor are more likely to be lifted. A moisture map checks the area under and beside the cabinets.

Do you handle the kitchen remodel afterward?

We handle drying, removal, cleanup and rebuild coordination. The cabinets, countertops and finishes are installed by rebuild trades, and we help line up the scope so the work follows the drying. The next stage is covered under water damage repair.
