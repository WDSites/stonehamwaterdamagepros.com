Water categories, in plain words

Category 1 water comes from a clean source, like a supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, such as a washer discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated: sewage, river water, or anything that sat long enough to turn. ANSI/IICRC S500 treats these differently, and porous materials like carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that touched Category 3 water are usually removed, not dried.

That is why flood damage restoration in Medford starts with identifying the source. A crew in protective gear extracts the water, removes contaminated porous material, cleans and disinfects hard surfaces, then dries what remains with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. Never run a vacuum or fan through sewage yourself.

Safety first

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Close the line that is leaking

Know the main shutoff before you need it. If the leak is an appliance, close the supply valve beside it. For sewage, stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer.

Dry footing or no breaker work

Turn off breakers only when you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If the panel is in the wet area, call 911 or the utility and wait outside.

Raise it out of reach

Move electronics, paperwork and soft items to counters, beds or an upper floor. Leave heavy furniture and appliances where they are.

Gloves, boots and a closed door

Dirty water is a health risk. Keep children and pets out, wear boots if you must enter, and wash your hands after any contact.

Insurance, rivers and what to ask

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowner policy. Flood damage is usually excluded without separate flood coverage, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Sewer backup may need its own endorsement, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

What does the city's 48-hour overflow notice mean for me?

It says to avoid contact with river water for 48 hours after a combined sewer overflow discharge ends. That is about swimming, wading or touching river water. It does not say a discharge causes basement backups. If your basement has sewage, treat it as Category 3 regardless.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

Small surface spills on hard floors can sometimes be handled with protective gear, but anything that soaked carpet, drywall or insulation needs professional extraction and removal. Sewage carries bacteria. Keep others out, and ask a physician about any health concern.
