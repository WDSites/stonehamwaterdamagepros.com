A drip in the bedroom ceiling is rarely the whole story. By the time water shows on drywall, it has usually soaked attic insulation, run along a rafter and spread across the back of the ceiling for a while. Here is the split of work. A roofer finds and fixes the opening, whether that is lifted shingles, bad flashing or a failed skylight seal. We handle the inside: extraction, removal of wet materials, drying and cleanup.

How roof water gets into a New England house

Winter does most of the damage here. Snow melts on the warm part of the roof, runs to the cold eave and refreezes into an ice dam. Meltwater then backs up under the shingles and drips into the wall tops and ceiling below. Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan describes snow and blizzards as a roughly once-a-year event, so the cycle repeats on most roofs in town.

Other entry points are chimney flashing, skylight seams, plumbing vent boots and roof valleys. After a strong nor'easter, shingles can lift or tear loose, and rain gets under them for hours before anyone looks up.

What you see and what it means

Water follows a rafter or a nail, then drops where the ceiling gives it a place to go. The stain can sit several feet from the true entry point.

What you see What it usually means
Brown ring on the ceiling that grows after rain Water is collecting in the cavity above and the drywall is wicking it
Bubbled or sagging paint Trapped water behind the surface, sometimes a lot of it
Drips from a recessed light or fan Water is following the wiring path, which raises an electrical concern
Dark patches or frost on attic sheathing Roof deck is wet, from a leak or from trapped humidity
Musty smell in a closet below the roof Insulation and framing have been damp for days, not hours

Safety first: electricity and ceilings

Water and a ceiling light fixture are a bad mix. If water is dripping from a fixture, a fan or a recessed light, leave that circuit alone. Flip that breaker off only from dry footing. A wet panel, a gas odor or visible sparks means you should leave the house and phone the utility or 911 ahead of any restoration call.

Wet drywall is heavy, and a bulging ceiling can let go of its load all at once. Keep people and pets out from under it and place a bucket under the lowest point.

Right now: what to do in the first hours

  1. Protect what is underneath

    Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the drip zone. Lay plastic over what cannot move.

  2. Contain the drip

    Set buckets or tubs below active leaks. Pinholing a small bulge to drain it into a bucket is a job for a crew, so call before you try it.

  3. Photograph everything

    Take wide and close photos of every stain, the attic if you can reach it safely, and the weather conditions. Stay off a wet or icy roof entirely.

  4. Call a roofer and the restoration line

    A roofer can tarp or patch the opening. We arrange extraction and drying so the wet materials do not sit.

Our process for the inside of the house

Crews start by mapping the water. A moisture meter tells them which drywall, plaster, framing and insulation are wet, and a thermal imaging camera shows cooler wet areas behind surfaces without cutting them open. Wet attic insulation is usually bagged and removed because it holds water against the ceiling joists and cannot dry in place.

Sagging, soft or stained-through ceiling drywall is cut out in controlled sections. Lighter damage may need only small holes, with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers drying the cavity. Daily readings show whether the framing is nearing the dry standard. HEPA air scrubbers and containment barriers keep dust out of clean rooms.

The ceiling itself gets patched and finished during ceiling water damage repair, which begins after everything is dry. If the roof leak was part of a larger weather event, storm water damage covers other ways wind and rain reach a house.

Trees, wind and the roof

According to Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan, wind can bring trees and power lines down, and Eversource cuts back branches on a routine cycle of roughly four to five years. Between those trims, a limb can land on your roof. If a limb has landed on the house, report any downed line to the utility before you go near it. The roof damage is a separate job for a roofer.

What changes the cost

You hear the scope and the price before any work begins. Several things move the number: how many rooms and floors got wet, how much attic insulation comes out, and whether the water was clean snowmelt or ran through dirty insulation. The ceiling material (drywall or plaster) and how much is cut out matter too, along with the drying days and equipment. The rebuild afterward is a separate phase.

Insurance and roof leaks

Policies tend to separate sudden storm damage from wear and long-term seepage. A wind event that opens the roof is often treated differently from a slow leak that has run for months. Flood damage is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage. Check your policy, ask your insurer before you throw items out, and keep your photos and receipts.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not paint over a stain before the cavity is dry, climb onto a wet or icy roof to look for the hole, or leave soaked attic insulation in place because the ceiling looks fine. If a musty smell turns into visible growth, see mold remediation and fix the water source first.

Ask the Stoneham Building Department whether the roof repair or rebuild needs a permit. The department's page does not say when permits apply to water damage work, so call the office at 35 Central Street, Garden Level. Permit applications go through the town's online portal.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency roof leak water damage cleanup available in Stoneham?

Yes. Someone picks up at any hour, weekends and holidays included, and a crew heads out the same day if one is open, though no arrival time is promised. Tell us where water is coming in, what is wet and whether any electrical fixtures are involved, and we will walk you through what to protect.

Do you repair the roof?

No. A roofer handles shingles, flashing, skylights and chimney work. What we send out is a restoration crew to pump out water, strip wet materials, dry the framing and clean the rooms below.

Can wet attic insulation be dried in place?

Sometimes, if it was lightly damp and the water was clean. Most often, soaked batt or blown insulation is removed, since it holds water against the ceiling and framing for a long time. A crew reads the insulation and the framing with moisture meters before making that call.

Will my insurance cover a roof leak?

Coverage turns on your policy and what caused the leak. Sudden storm damage is often treated differently from gradual leaks or wear, and flood is usually excluded. Check your policy, ask your insurer what documentation it wants, and keep photographs of the damage and the weather.

Should I pop a bulging ceiling?

Leave it alone. Behind that bulge may be gallons of water, and the board can drop on you. Stand clear, set a bucket beneath it and call so a crew can drain it under control.
