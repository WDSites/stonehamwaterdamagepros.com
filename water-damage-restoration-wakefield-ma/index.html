The freeze, thaw and storm cycle in a Wakefield house

Cold weather hurts plumbing in predictable places: exterior walls, garage ceilings, crawl spaces, the run behind a kitchen sink on an outside wall. A pipe can crack on a freezing night and stay quiet because the ice is acting as a plug. When it thaws, pressure returns and water pours out of a hairline split.

If you hear running water in a wall after a cold spell, shut the main and call. Our water mitigation crews trace the wetness with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry cavities before mold gets a start.

When water is spreading in Wakefield, look at the lake and the pavement

Around Lake Quannapowitt and Wakefield Common, as anywhere in town, street drains carry stormwater away. If those drains are overwhelmed, water can rise into basement floor drains. Water that comes from a drain or a sewer line is Category 3, so stay out and call before touching anything.

Our emergency water removal service handles the pump-out. Extraction units pull standing water first, then crews check carpet pad, baseboards and the bottom of wall cavities for wetness. Wood subfloor and framing dry slower than drywall, and readings guide when equipment can come out.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Turn off what feeds it

A burst pipe means closing the main. A washer or toilet leak means the little valve beside it. Water still running overhead keeps feeding the ceiling below.

Power: only if the floor is dry

Flip breakers for the wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. If it is in a wet basement, leave it alone and ring the utility or 911.

Rescue what can move

Take rugs, boxes, shoes, cords and anything cardboard out of the wet zone. Photograph the damage as you go, since your insurer will ask.

Stay out of mystery water

If it came from a drain or has an odor, treat it as contaminated. Keep family and pets away from it and do not wade in barefoot.

What the Wakefield DPW tells you, and how to use it

Wakefield's DPW is at 1 Lafayette Street and runs a sewer infiltration and inflow reduction program. That work targets groundwater and stormwater getting into the sanitary sewer, which can overload the system in heavy rain. The DPW also posts sanitary sewer overflow notifications, so if sewage is backing up, check the notices and report it to the DPW.

The DPW also lists drainage improvement projects on Cedar Court, Cedar Street and Emerson Street. If you live on or near those streets, ask the DPW about project status before assuming a repeat flood is your plumbing. We dry the damage. The DPW works on the pipes in the street.

About 85 percent of Wakefield's water comes from the MWRA through two connections, and about 15 percent comes from the town's Broadway Treatment and Pumping Facility, which draws on Crystal Lake. For you, that means a street-wide pressure or taste change is a call to the DPW first.

What should I do if a pipe froze but has not burst?

Open the faucet slightly, add gentle heat with a hair dryer or space heater placed safely away from water, and keep the cabinet doors open. Never use a flame. If the pipe is bulging or you hear water inside the wall after it thaws, shut the main and call.

How do I know if sewage in my basement is a town sewer problem?

Check the DPW's sanitary sewer overflow notifications and call the DPW at 1 Lafayette Street. Whatever the cause, sewage is Category 3. Stay out, keep the washer off, and call us for cleanup. Ask your insurer how backup is covered under your policy.

Why does the DPW mention infiltration and inflow?

Infiltration is groundwater leaking into sewer pipes, and inflow is stormwater entering through connections. Both add volume during storms. Reducing them helps the system cope. It does not remove your own risk, so test your sump pump and know where your basement drain goes.

Do the Cedar Court, Cedar Street and Emerson Street projects affect my claim?

We cannot say how a project affects any claim. Describe the damage, take photos, and ask your insurer. Ask the DPW what work is planned or underway on your street, and keep your own records of when water entered.
