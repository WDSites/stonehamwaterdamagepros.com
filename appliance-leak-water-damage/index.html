Appliance Leaks

Appliance Leak Water Damage in Stoneham, MA

Washers, dishwashers and ice maker lines fail quietly. We dry what got wet and clean up the rest, once the source is shut off.

A washing machine hose lets go while you are at work, and by the time you get home the laundry room floor is covered and water has found the ceiling below. That is the loud version of an appliance leak. The quiet version is a dishwasher gasket that has been weeping into a subfloor for two months, and nobody knows until a floor feels soft.

Where appliance leaks come from

Most appliance leaks start at a connection, not in the machine itself. Washer supply hoses are rubber or braided lines that sit under constant pressure, and they split at the fittings. Drain hoses slip out of standpipes. Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, the inlet valve and the drain loop. Refrigerator ice maker lines are thin plastic or copper tubes that crack, loosen or get pinched when the fridge is pushed back.

Category matters. A supply line leak is clean water, category 1. A washer or dishwasher drain leak carries soap, food and lint, which makes it category 2. That decides whether carpet and pad can be saved.

What you are seeing and what it means

Appliance leaks announce themselves in small ways. Read the clues before you decide how big the problem is.

What you notice What it usually means
Floor feels spongy near the washer or dishwasher Subfloor has been wet for days or weeks
Vinyl or laminate edges lifting Water is spreading under the flooring
Musty smell in the laundry room or kitchen Damp material is hidden behind a wall or under the floor
Brown ring or bubble on the ceiling below Water has traveled through the floor system
Baseboard swelling beside the appliance Drywall at the base is wicking water upward
Water bill climbs with no change in habits A line is leaking under pressure somewhere

Upstairs laundry leaks follow joist bays downward, so the room below can be wetter than the laundry room.

Safety first: the first hour

Start at the appliance. Turn off the water at the small shut-off valves behind or beside it. If you cannot find them, close the main water valve for the house. Pull the plug only if the outlet and the floor around it are dry.

If water is near outlets, a power strip or the panel, stay out of the area and cut power only if you can get to the panel with dry ground under your feet. Sparking, a gas smell or any other electrical or gas risk means the utility or 911 hears from you first and our line second. Gas dryers and ranges sit near many laundry and kitchen leaks, so smell for gas before anything else.

Then photograph the damage and call a plumber or appliance technician. We do not repair appliances or plumbing. We handle the water damage left behind.

  1. Moisture mapping

    Meters and a thermal imaging camera trace where the water went beyond the visible spot.

  2. Extraction

    Extraction units pull standing water and wring water out of carpet and pad. Wet pad from category 2 water is usually removed.

  3. Controlled removal

    Swollen baseboard, soft underlayment and wet drywall at the base are cut out so the cavity can breathe. Dry flooring can often stay.

  4. Drying

    Drying equipment stays until readings equal the dry standard in an unaffected room of your own home. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are checked daily.

  5. Treatment and cleanup

    Surfaces that stay get an antimicrobial treatment, and debris is removed.

Slow leaks and why they cost more

Subfloor plywood and floor joists take on water gradually, and a dishwasher or ice maker line can keep them damp for weeks. Wet wood that stays wet gives mold a place to grow, and growth can begin on damp materials in about two days.

After weeks of seepage, drying alone may not be enough, and rotted subfloor sections may need replacing. If mold is visible, see mold remediation before anything is sealed back up. Fix the leak first, because mold needs moisture.

Insurance: sudden versus gradual

Policies tend to separate sudden and accidental water damage from damage that built up over time. A hose that bursts on a Tuesday is often treated differently from a gasket that leaked for months. Flood coverage is a separate product and does not apply here. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Dated photos, the failed part and moisture readings all help.

Frequently asked questions

Is a washing machine hose leak covered by insurance?

It depends on your policy. A hose that fails all at once is often handled as a sudden loss, while a slow leak over many weeks often is not. Go over the wording with your insurer, and save dated photos along with the failed hose.

How long does it take to dry a floor after a dishwasher leak?

Expect several days in many cases, with the length set by materials and depth. Plywood subfloor and framing dry slower than the surface. Crews take daily readings and stop when wet areas match the dry standard in an unaffected part of your home, so no day count is promised.

Can I just replace the flooring and skip the drying?

Not safely. New flooring over a damp subfloor traps moisture, which can warp the floor and feed mold. Drying comes first, confirmed with a moisture meter.

What if the leak is behind the refrigerator?

Shut off the water to the ice maker line and unplug the fridge only if the outlet is dry. Water often spreads under nearby cabinets, so ask for a moisture check of the toe kick and cabinet base too.

Do you fix the appliance or the supply line?

No. A plumber or appliance technician repairs the hose, valve or machine. We extract water, dry the structure, clean up and coordinate rebuilding. Have the leak repaired first, because drying while water still flows only adds to the damage.
