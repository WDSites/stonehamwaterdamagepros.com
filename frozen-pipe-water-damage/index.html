Frozen Pipe Damage

Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Stoneham, MA

The split happens when the pipe freezes, but the flood starts when it thaws. Here is what to do before and after the ice melts.

Call (339) 333-9345

Nothing looks wrong while a pipe is frozen. The water inside has turned to ice, the ice has stretched the pipe wall, and the split sits there closed until the temperature rises. Then the ice melts, pressure returns and water pours from a crack you could not see an hour earlier.

That timing is the whole story of frozen pipe damage. The damage shows up at thaw, often when the heat comes back, the power returns or the weather turns, and that is when you should have a plan ready. If water is already flowing, call (339) 333-9345 after you shut off the main.

Why the damage shows up at thaw

Ice does not usually break a pipe at the frozen spot. It builds pressure between the ice plug and the closed faucet, and the weakest section of pipe splits. The crack can be a thin slit along a copper pipe or a burst fitting.

Pipes that fail from wear or a bad fitting are covered on the burst pipe water damage page. This page is about the freeze case, where the cause is cold and the failure is delayed.

Where pipes freeze in a New England house

The usual suspects are pipes in cold places. Exterior walls with thin insulation, unheated garages, hose bibs that still have a hose attached, bump-outs over open space, crawl spaces under additions and basement rim joist areas near drafts all run cold. Sink lines on outside walls are common trouble spots, because cabinet doors keep warm air away from the pipe.

Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan rates extreme cold as "serious" for the town, and says snow and blizzards arrive about once a year. A cold snap is a normal part of the season. If your home has a pipe in any of the places above, treat the first hard freeze of the year as your cue to check them.

Frozen pipe right now: what to do before the thaw

If a faucet runs slowly or not at all in cold weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Act before it thaws.

Safety first: no flames, no extension cords in water

Never use a torch, a propane heater or an open flame on a pipe. Do not use electrical heaters or hair dryers near standing water. If water has reached outlets or the panel, keep out of the room. Electrical or gas trouble means calling the utility or 911, then us.

Open the faucet on the frozen line so water has a place to go as the ice melts. Shut off the main water valve if you suspect a split, then leave it closed. Open cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes. Use a hair dryer on a low setting only if the area is dry and the cord is not near water. Call a plumber to check the line before turning the water back on.

Leave the water off until a plumber checks

A pipe that held while frozen may still be cracked. Turning the water back on can send water through a hidden split into a wall or ceiling cavity. Keep the main closed until a plumber has looked at every exposed run and every fitting near the frozen spot.

A plumber repairs or replaces the pipe. We do not do plumbing. We dispatch restoration professionals for the water that already got out, including wet cavities behind walls.

Power outages and a freeze

Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan names wind-felled trees and power lines as a local concern and flags branches hanging over lines as a known problem. Eversource trims on a schedule of roughly every four to five years, so a storm can still cut power between visits.

When the power goes out, the furnace stops, and some rooms can drop below freezing within hours. If you lose heat in a cold snap, leave a faucet dripping and watch the first rooms that go cold. When power returns, the pipes thaw, so walk the house and check for new sounds, stains or drips.

What crews do after a thaw

  1. Shut off and survey

    The water is closed at the main, then a crew scans the wet areas using moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, looking for water inside walls and ceilings.

  2. Extract standing water

    Extraction units remove water from floors and carpet, and wet pad is lifted or removed.

  3. Open and dry the cavities

    Wet insulation and soaked drywall are cut out. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on the remaining structure.

  4. Monitor and clean

    Crews return to read moisture levels until the structure matches dry areas of your home, then treat surfaces with an antimicrobial.

The guide to water mitigation explains how the first days of drying work. If water is still pooling, emergency water removal covers the pump-out stage.

Insurance and permits

A pipe that splits in a cold snap is often treated as sudden damage, but freeze wording varies from policy to policy, so read yours and ask your insurer. Slow leaks and neglect are the usual exclusions, and rising water from outside the plumbing needs separate flood coverage.

Permit questions for the rebuild belong with the Building Department in the Garden Level of 35 Central Street. Filing happens in the town's online permit portal, and inspections are booked by calling the office. Nothing on the department's page spells out whether a water damage repair needs a permit, so ask first.

Prevention before the next freeze

Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. Disconnect hoses from outdoor spigots before the cold arrives. Keep the heat on, even if you travel. Know where your main shut-off is and test it once a year.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency frozen pipe water damage available in Stoneham?

Yes. Dispatch answers day and night, holidays and weekends included. Tell us what thawed and what is wet, and we will talk you through steps while a crew is arranged. A visit the same day is possible if a crew is open, but we cannot promise a time.

Why did my pipe leak only after it thawed?

Ice can hold a split closed. The crack opens when the ice melts and pressure returns. That is why many frozen pipe floods start hours after the weather warms or the heat comes back on.

Should I thaw the pipe myself?

You can, carefully. Open the faucet, use a hair dryer on low in a dry area, and never use flames. If you see a crack, leave the water off and call a plumber.

Can I turn the water back on after thawing?

Not until a plumber checks the line. A pipe that survived may still have a hidden split, and turning the water on can flood a wall cavity. Keep the main closed until the plumber says the system is sound.

How do I know water is hiding inside a wall?

Soft or cold drywall, stains, bubbling paint, a musty smell and rising bills are common clues. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meters can confirm wet cavities that cannot be seen from the room.
