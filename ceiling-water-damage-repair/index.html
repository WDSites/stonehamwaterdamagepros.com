Ceiling Repair

Water Damaged Ceiling Repair in Stoneham, MA

Stained, bubbled or sagging ceilings need the trapped water relieved, the wet drywall removed and the cavity dried before anyone paints.

Call (339) 333-9345

A ceiling holds more than you would think. Drywall and plaster act like a lid, and when water lands on top of them, the lid keeps it there until the paint bulges or the board sags. By the time you see the stain, the cavity above has often been wet for a while.

This page is about repairing the ceiling itself. If the water is still coming from the roof, start with roof leak water damage, because a ceiling patched under an active leak will fail again. If you see fuzzy or dark growth, mold remediation handles that part, after the moisture source is fixed.

What you see and what is going on behind it

A brown stain with a hard edge usually means water came through, dried, and may have come back more than once. A soft, bubbled or sagging patch means water is still trapped inside. Stains that spread after a shower upstairs point to a tub, toilet or supply line. Stains that spread after rain or snowmelt point to the roof.

Recessed lights deserve special attention. Water collects in the can above the trim, then drips along wiring. A dripping fixture is an electrical issue as well as a wet ceiling.

Ceilings under upstairs bathrooms are another common case. A failed tub seal or toilet wax ring can drip into the joist bay for weeks before the board below is saturated.

Safety before you touch the ceiling

Stay out from under a sagging ceiling. Wet drywall can weigh many times its dry weight, and it can drop without warning. Keep children and pets in another room and put a bucket under the lowest point.

If water is coming out of a light fixture, fan or recessed can, do not flip the switch. Kill that circuit at the panel, but only when the floor around the panel is dry. A burning smell, gas odor, sparks or a damp panel call for the utility or 911 first, and the restoration line second. Shut off the water supply to the fixture upstairs if you know where it is.

How a ceiling gets repaired

The order of work matters, and skipping a step is how stains come back.

  1. Stop the source

    A plumber, roofer or HVAC technician fixes the leak first. We do not repair plumbing, roofs or air conditioners.

  2. Relieve trapped water

    A crew pokes a controlled relief hole in the lowest point of the bulge, catches the water and protects the floor below.

  3. Cut out what is wet

    Sagging, soft or stained-through drywall or plaster is cut back to dry, solid material. Wet insulation above it is bagged and removed.

  4. Dry the cavity

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map the wet area. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the joists and the space above the ceiling until the readings match unaffected areas of your home.

  5. Prime and rebuild

    Once everything is dry, new board is hung, taped and finished, a stain-blocking primer seals the area and the texture is matched before painting.

Drywall, plaster and texture

Drywall that only has a light stain and is firm to the touch can sometimes stay once it has dried and been sealed. Board that is sagging, crumbling or has lost its paper face is replaced. Plaster ceilings in older houses are harder to read, since plaster can loosen from the lath as it dries.

Stain-blocking primer matters because ordinary paint lets water stains bleed through. Matching the old texture, whether flat, knockdown or popcorn, is part of the finish work, and a patched area often needs a full ceiling coat to blend. Older textured ceilings may contain materials that need a lab test before sanding or scraping, so ask before anyone disturbs them.

Why cavity drying matters

A closed ceiling cavity is a poor place to dry. Air does not move, so wet insulation and joists stay damp for weeks. Opening the cavity, even with a few holes, lets air movers push dry air through. That keeps a repainted ceiling from staining again.

Stoneham's hazard mitigation plan notes a Middlesex County shift: heavy rain or snow events once came roughly yearly around the 1950s and now show up about every nine months. That gives water more chances for water to find a way in, so recheck a repaired ceiling after the next heavy storm and watch for a new ring.

What changes the cost

You get a scope and a price up front. Both depend on how much ceiling is wet, whether it is drywall or plaster, whether insulation has to be removed, and the height and access of the room. Drying days and equipment count too, and so does how much finishing is required, including texture matching and painting.

Insurance, in general terms

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe failure or a storm opening the roof, is often covered under homeowner policies. Slow leaks and long-term seepage often are not. Rising floodwater is a separate matter and generally needs its own flood policy. Read your policy, and ask the insurer which records it wants before any materials are torn out. Photograph the ceiling before anything is cut.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not paint over a wet stain, because trapped moisture will blister the new paint. Do not leave soaked insulation above the ceiling. Do not screw a sagging board back up and call it done.

Before rebuilding, call the town's Building Department (35 Central Street, Garden Level) to ask whether a permit applies. Its published page is silent on that question for water damage work. Applications are filed in the online portal.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sagging ceiling be saved?

Sometimes, if the board is firm and the water was clean. A ceiling that is visibly sagging usually has too much water trapped in it, and the drywall has lost strength, so it is typically cut out. A crew checks with a moisture meter before deciding.

Is it safe to poke a hole in a bulging ceiling?

No, leave that to a crew. The board can come down as you cut, and the water behind it may be considerable. Professionals drain it from the lowest point with floors covered below. Stay clear and call if in doubt.

How long until a wet ceiling is dry?

It depends on the materials and how much water got in. Drying a cavity is measured by readings from moisture meters, not by a set number of days. A crew checks daily and compares readings to unaffected parts of your home. No one can promise a fixed number of days.

Do you paint and finish the ceiling?

Restoration professionals handle the removal, drying and cleanup, and rebuild coordination is part of the job. Finish work, including taping, texture and paint, is planned as a separate repair phase so it begins only after the cavity is dry. See water damage repair for how rebuilding is scheduled.

Does a stained ceiling mean there is mold?

Not always, but damp materials can grow mold within about 48 hours. Stains alone do not tell you. Dark spots, a musty smell or a ceiling that stays damp are reasons to have it checked. Mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520, and health questions belong to a physician.
